## Region-Specific Mobility Factors

### Mountain Terrain as Primary Barrier
**CRITICAL:** Mountains are not just scenic features—they are hard barriers that prevent routine healthcare access.

**Major Mountain Ranges:**
- **Rocky Mountains (CO, WY, MT, ID):** Create north-south corridors, severe east-west barriers
- **Wasatch Range (UT):** Separates Salt Lake City from eastern Utah
- **Sierra Nevada (NV/CA border):** Isolates western Nevada from California
- **Sangre de Cristo Mountains (CO/NM):** Separate Front Range from San Luis Valley
- **Continental Divide:** Runs through CO, MT, NM creating fundamental east-west split

**Mountain Passes and Winter Closures:**
- **I-70 through Colorado Rockies:** Frequent closures, 2-4 hour delays common
- **Loveland Pass, Eisenhower Tunnel:** Bottlenecks between Denver and Western Slope
- **Independence Pass (CO):** Closed November-May (6 months)
- **Beartooth Highway (MT/WY):** Closed October-May
- **Logan Canyon (UT):** Winter avalanche risk between Cache Valley and Wasatch Front
- **Monida Pass (MT/ID border):** I-15 corridor, winter closures
- **Wolf Creek Pass (CO):** US-160 between San Luis Valley and Southwest CO

**Elevation as Barrier:**
- **High altitude reduces willingness to drive:** 10,000+ ft passes create fatigue, weather risk
- **Winter driving:** 6-8 months of snow/ice in mountain corridors
- **Residents normalize long drives on flat terrain but avoid mountain crossings**

### Extreme Distances in Frontier States
**Montana, Wyoming, Nevada, Idaho, New Mexico** have some of the lowest population densities in the continental US.

**Distance Characteristics:**
- **100+ miles between towns is common**
- **200-400 miles between regional hubs is normal**
- **Montana east-west:** Billings to Missoula = 340 miles (5+ hours)
- **Nevada north-south:** Reno to Las Vegas = 450 miles (7+ hours)
- **Wyoming:** Casper to Jackson = 280 miles (5+ hours)
- **New Mexico:** Albuquerque to Farmington = 180 miles (3+ hours)

**However:** The 45-minute rule still applies for routine care. Do NOT consolidate just because residents accept long drives for specialty care.

### Limited Transit Infrastructure
**CRITICAL:** This region is entirely car-dependent for healthcare access. Transit does NOT materially reduce friction.

**RTD (Denver):**
- Light rail serves limited Denver metro corridors
- Does NOT extend to major suburban hospital clusters (Boulder, Fort Collins, Colorado Springs)
- Does NOT collapse Front Range markets

**UTA (Salt Lake City):**
- TRAX light rail serves Salt Lake Valley
- FrontRunner commuter rail connects Ogden-Salt Lake-Provo
- May provide SOME friction reduction within Wasatch Front but does NOT collapse all markets
- Mountain barriers to Park City, eastern Utah remain absolute

**All other metros:** Zero meaningful transit for healthcare purposes

### Air Travel as Specialty Care Alternative
**Unique to this region:** Residents sometimes fly to regional hubs for tertiary care rather than drive across mountains.

**Examples:**
- Gunnison/Crested Butte (CO) → Denver (direct flights vs 4+ hour mountain drive)
- Jackson Hole (WY) → Salt Lake City (direct flights vs 5 hour mountain drive)
- Kalispell (MT) → Seattle or Denver (regional medical flights)

**However:** This does NOT integrate markets for routine care. Air travel is for tertiary/quaternary care only.

### Weather and Seasonal Access
- **Winter:** 6-8 months of snow in mountain areas, 4-5 months in Front Range
- **Spring runoff:** Road closures, flooding in valleys
- **Wildfire season:** Summer smoke and evacuations affect access
- **Altitude sickness:** Reduces willingness to travel to high-elevation areas

### Ski Resort Economies
Unique healthcare challenges:
- **Seasonal population spikes:** Aspen, Vail, Park City, Jackson Hole, Sun Valley
- **Year-round healthcare infrastructure:** These are real markets despite small permanent populations
- **Medical evacuation patterns:** Helicopter transport to Denver, Salt Lake common
