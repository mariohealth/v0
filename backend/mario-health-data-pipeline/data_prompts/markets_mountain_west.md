## Region-Specific Mobility Factors

### Mountain Terrain as Primary Barrier
**CRITICAL:** Mountains are not just scenic features—they are hard barriers that prevent routine healthcare access.

**Major Mountain Ranges:**
- **Rocky Mountains (CO, WY, MT, ID):** Create north-south corridors, severe east-west barriers
- **Wasatch Range (UT):** Separates Salt Lake City from eastern Utah
- **Sierra Nevada (NV/CA border):** Isolates western Nevada from California
- **Sangre de Cristo Mountains (CO/NM):** Separate Front Range from San Luis Valley
- **Continental Divide:** Runs through CO, MT, NM creating fundamental east-west split
- **Mogollon Rim (AZ):** Separates Phoenix lowlands from northern Arizona highlands

**Mountain Passes and Winter Closures:**
- **I-70 through Colorado Rockies:** Frequent closures, 2-4 hour delays common
- **Loveland Pass, Eisenhower Tunnel:** Bottlenecks between Denver and Western Slope
- **Independence Pass (CO):** Closed November-May (6 months)
- **Beartooth Highway (MT/WY):** Closed October-May
- **Logan Canyon (UT):** Winter avalanche risk between Cache Valley and Wasatch Front
- **Monida Pass (MT/ID border):** I-15 corridor, winter closures
- **Wolf Creek Pass (CO):** US-160 between San Luis Valley and Southwest CO

**Elevation as Barrier:**
- **High altitude reduces willingness to drive:** 10,000+ ft passes create fatigue, weather risk
- **Winter driving:** 6-8 months of snow/ice in mountain corridors
- **Residents normalize long drives on flat terrain but avoid mountain crossings**
