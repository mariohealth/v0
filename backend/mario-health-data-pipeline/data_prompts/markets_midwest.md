## Region-Specific Mobility Factors

### Winter Weather as Major Friction Factor
**CRITICAL:** Winter materially increases travel friction in this region (November through March, 5 months).

**Winter Impact:**
- **Snow and ice:** Frequent storms create hazardous driving conditions
- **Lake effect snow:** Particularly severe near Great Lakes
  - Buffalo (NY, not in this region but illustrative): 95" annual snowfall
  - Milwaukee: 47" annual snowfall
  - Cleveland: 68" annual snowfall
  - Chicago: 38" annual snowfall
- **Sub-zero temperatures:** January averages: Chicago 23°F, Minneapolis 13°F, Detroit 25°F
- **Cold aversion:** Residents avoid long drives in extreme cold
- **Road closures:** I-90, I-94, I-80 corridors affected by winter storms

**Result:** Winter reduces willingness to drive 60+ miles for routine care. The 45-minute rule becomes MORE binding in winter, not less.

### Great Lakes as Absolute Water Barriers

**Lake Michigan (Critical for Chicago/Milwaukee/Michigan):**
- **No direct crossings** — Chicago to Michigan requires driving around southern tip (90-120 miles)
- **Ferry service:** Milwaukee ↔ Muskegon (seasonal only, 2.5 hour crossing, not practical for routine care)
- **Result:** Chicago metro and Southeast WI are completely separate from Michigan markets
- **Gary IN** may be integrated with Chicago metro or separate

**Lake Erie (Critical for Cleveland/Detroit):**
- **No direct crossings** between Cleveland OH and Detroit MI
- **Toledo OH acts as land bridge** but is 60 miles from Cleveland and 60 miles from Detroit
- **Result:** Cleveland and Detroit are completely separate markets despite same lake

**Lake Superior:**
- **Northern MN isolation:** Duluth separated from Twin Cities by 150+ miles
- **No crossings to Michigan Upper Peninsula**
- **Houghton/Marquette MI** isolated from Lower Peninsula

**Lake Huron / Lake St. Clair:**
- **Separate Michigan Lower Peninsula from Ontario**
- **Detroit-Windsor tunnel/bridge:** International crossing, not used for routine US healthcare

**Lake Winnebago (Wisconsin):**
- Minor barrier but affects access in Fox Valley region

### Urban Congestion (Moderate by National Standards)

**Chicago:**
- **I-90/I-94 Dan Ryan/Kennedy:** Chronic congestion through city core
- **I-290 Eisenhower:** East-west bottleneck
- **I-294 Tri-State Tollway:** Ring road around metro, moderate congestion
- **I-55 Stevenson:** Southwest corridor congestion
- **Lake Michigan forces crescent shape:** Creates 60-90+ min crossing times for east-west travel
- **Expect 6-10 distinct markets within Chicago CSA**

**Detroit:**
- **I-75 / I-94 / I-96 convergence:** Downtown bottleneck
- **I-275 / I-696 ring roads:** Suburban sprawl
- **Ambassador Bridge / Detroit-Windsor Tunnel:** International, not relevant for routine care
- **Expect 3-5 distinct markets within Detroit metro**

**Minneapolis-St. Paul:**
- **I-94 corridor:** Connects two city cores (10 miles apart)
- **I-494 / I-694 ring roads:** Create suburban sprawl
- **Mississippi River:** Minimal barrier (many bridges)
- **Expect 2-3 markets** (may be integrated or split depending on systems)

**Other Metros:**
- Cleveland, Columbus, Cincinnati, Indianapolis, Milwaukee: Moderate congestion, likely single markets each

### Terrain (Mostly Flat, Minimal Barriers)
- **No major mountain ranges** in this region
- **Glacial plains:** Generally flat terrain
- **River crossings:** Ohio River (Cincinnati/Louisville), Mississippi River (Quad Cities, Twin Cities)
- **Minor hills:** Southern OH, Southern IN (Appalachian foothills)

**Result:** Terrain is NOT a major barrier (unlike Mountain West or Pacific Northwest). Distance and weather are primary barriers.
