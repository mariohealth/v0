## Region-Specific Mobility Factors

### Water Barriers (Dominant Factor in Puget Sound)
**CRITICAL:** Puget Sound creates the most significant water barriers outside of New York area.

**Lake Washington:**
- **I-90 floating bridge**: Connects Seattle to Eastside (Bellevue, Redmond)
- **SR-520 floating bridge**: North connection to Eastside
- Bridges create 15-30 min friction but may NOT fully separate markets if transit/bridges sufficient

**Puget Sound Crossings:**
- **Tacoma Narrows Bridge (SR-16)**: Connects Kitsap Peninsula to Tacoma
- **Ferry system**: Multiple routes but ferry schedules create HARD barriers
  - Seattle ↔ Bainbridge Island (35 min ferry)
  - Seattle ↔ Bremerton (60 min ferry)
  - Edmonds ↔ Kingston
  - Mukilteo ↔ Clinton (Whidbey Island)
- **Ferry-dependent communities are separate markets**

**Columbia River (WA/OR border):**
- **I-5 bridges** (Portland-Vancouver): May integrate Portland metro across state line
- **I-205 bridges**: East Portland to Vancouver WA
- **Decision:** Evaluate if Portland-Vancouver WA is single integrated market or separate

### Cascade Mountains (Absolute East-West Barrier)
**CRITICAL:** The Cascade Range creates a fundamental divide between Western and Eastern Washington/Oregon.

**Mountain Passes:**
- **Snoqualmie Pass (I-90)**: Seattle to Eastern WA, winter closures, 3,000+ ft elevation
- **Stevens Pass (US-2)**: Everett area to Eastern WA, frequent winter closures
- **White Pass (US-12)**: South-central crossing, limited capacity
- **Mount Hood passes**: Portland to Eastern OR, seasonal closures

**Result:** Western WA/OR and Eastern WA/OR are completely separate healthcare regions.

### Limited Transit (With Meaningful Impact in Seattle)

**Sounder (Seattle commuter rail):**
- **North Line**: Seattle ↔ Everett (Snohomish County)
- **South Line**: Seattle ↔ Tacoma (Pierce County)
- Primarily serves work commutes but stations near some medical facilities
- **May provide SOME friction reduction** within Puget Sound corridor

**Link Light Rail (Seattle):**
- Connects Seattle ↔ SeaTac Airport ↔ Tacoma (opening)
- Limited coverage, doesn't extend to all Eastside areas
- May provide SOME friction reduction where it exists

**MAX Light Rail (Portland):**
- Extensive coverage in Portland metro
- Crosses Columbia River to serve Vancouver WA
- **May enable Portland-Vancouver WA integration**

**All other areas:** Zero meaningful transit for healthcare purposes

### Congestion Corridors

**Seattle:**
- **I-5 corridor**: Chronic all-day congestion through Seattle/Tacoma
- **I-405 Eastside**: Ring road around Lake Washington, moderate congestion
- **SR-520 / I-90 bridges**: Lake crossing bottlenecks

**Portland:**
- **I-5 corridor**: Moderate congestion through metro
- **I-84 corridor**: East-west through Columbia Gorge
- **I-205**: Outer ring, moderate congestion

**Moderate by national standards but still creates friction.**

### Terrain Beyond Cascades

**Olympic Mountains:** Isolate Olympic Peninsula from Seattle
**Coast Range:** Create barriers along Oregon coast
**Columbia River Gorge:** Wind corridor, creates east-west friction in Portland area

### Weather and Seasonal Factors
- **Rain:** 6-9 months of rain reduces willingness for long drives
- **Mountain passes:** Winter closures isolate Eastern WA/OR from west
- **Fog:** Coastal and valley fog affects travel
- **Volcanic hazard:** Mount Rainier, Mount Hood (does not change market definitions)

The Seattle CSA is NOT a single market:
- Lake Washington may or may not separate Seattle from Eastside
