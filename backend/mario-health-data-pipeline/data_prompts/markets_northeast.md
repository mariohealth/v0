## Region-Specific Mobility Factors

### Major Congestion Corridors
- **I-95 corridor** (Boston to DC): Chronic congestion, especially through NYC, Providence, Stamford
- **I-287** (NY/NJ beltway): Heavy truck traffic, frequent delays
- **I-84** (Danbury to Hartford): Morning/evening peaks
- **Garden State Parkway / NJ Turnpike**: Tolls + congestion

### Bridge and Tunnel Bottlenecks
These create hard friction boundaries—expect queues 20-40 minutes during typical daytime hours:

**New York Region:**
- George Washington Bridge (Manhattan ↔ NJ)
- Lincoln/Holland Tunnels (Manhattan ↔ NJ)
- Verrazano Bridge (Brooklyn ↔ Staten Island)
- Throgs Neck/Whitestone Bridges (Bronx ↔ Queens)
- Limited East River crossings to Manhattan

**Other Critical Crossings:**
- Tappan Zee / Mario Cuomo Bridge (Westchester ↔ Rockland County)
- Tobin Bridge (Boston ↔ North Shore)
- Mount Hope Bridge (RI mainland ↔ Aquidneck Island)
- Delaware River crossings (PA ↔ NJ)

### Strong Commuter Rail Systems
These systems can reduce friction **when they directly serve medical districts:**

**MBTA (Boston):**
- Red Line: Cambridge ↔ Boston medical district (Longwood) — HIGH medical usage
- Orange/Green Lines: Connect to academic centers — MEDIUM medical usage
- Commuter Rail: Primarily work trips, poor medical access

**Metro-North (NY):**
- Grand Central to Westchester/CT — connects to NYC hospitals but requires subway transfer
- Does NOT collapse healthcare markets (peak-direction commuting only)

**NJ Transit:**
- Penn Station access — requires subway transfer to most hospitals
- Does NOT collapse markets (designed for work trips)

**LIRR (Long Island):**
- Penn Station access — requires transfer to reach most Manhattan hospitals
- Does NOT collapse markets (Long Island is separate healthcare market)

**SEPTA (Philadelphia):**
- Regional Rail to Center City — limited direct hospital access
- Market Center line serves University City medical campus

### Ferry Systems
These **materially reduce friction** in harbors:

**NYC:**
- Staten Island Ferry (free, 24/7, high frequency) — does reduce friction
- East River Ferry — marginal impact (expensive, limited hours)

**Boston:**
- Charlestown Ferry to downtown — minor impact
- Hull/Hingham ferries — primarily recreational

### Terrain and Weather
- White Mountains (NH): Creates hard barrier for east-west travel
- Green Mountains (VT): North-south travel only (I-91, Route 7)
- Adirondacks (NY): Isolates North Country from Capital Region
- Winter weather: VT/NH/Northern NY have 4-6 months of difficult driving conditions

The NYC CSA (23M people) is too large to be a single healthcare market. Expected splits:

**Required Markets (Minimum 6):**

3. **Queens** — NYC Health + Hospitals/Queens, Northwell presence
4. **Bronx** — Montefiore dominance
5. **Northern New Jersey** — Hackensack Meridian, RWJBarnabas, Atlantic Health
6. **Westchester County** — White Plains, New Rochelle (separate from Manhattan due to bridge friction)

8. **Hudson Valley** — Vassar Brothers (Poughkeepsie), separate from Westchester

**Reasoning:**
- East River crossings are bottlenecks (45+ min to Manhattan from outer boroughs)
- NJ has independent anchor systems (not NYC-dominated)
- Long Island: LIRR requires transfer, most routine care stays local
- Westchester: Tappan Zee + congestion creates 60+ min travel to Manhattan
