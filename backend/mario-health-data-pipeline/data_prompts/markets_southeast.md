## Region-Specific Mobility Factors

### Major Congestion Corridors
- **I-85 corridor** (Charlotte to Atlanta): Chronic congestion through both metros
- **I-285 (Atlanta Perimeter)**: Extreme congestion, 60-90+ min to cross during typical daytime
- **I-75** (Atlanta to Florida): Major north-south artery with truck-heavy traffic
- **I-95 corridor** (Florida coast): Chronic congestion through Jacksonville, Miami, Fort Lauderdale
- **I-4 corridor** (Tampa to Orlando to Daytona): Extreme congestion, major bottleneck for central Florida
- **I-26** (Charleston to Columbia to Asheville): Connects distinct markets, does NOT integrate them
- **I-40** (Tennessee): Knoxville to Nashville corridor with Smoky Mountains barrier

### Limited Transit Infrastructure
**CRITICAL:** The Southeast is almost entirely car-dependent for healthcare access. Transit does NOT materially reduce friction in this region.

**MARTA (Atlanta):**
- Limited coverage, primarily serves downtown/airport corridor
- Does NOT extend to major suburban hospital clusters (Gwinnett, Cobb, North Fulton)
- Does NOT collapse Atlanta suburban markets

**Tri-Rail (South Florida):**
- Coastal commuter rail, poor medical facility access
- Does NOT collapse Miami/Fort Lauderdale/West Palm markets

**Miami Metrorail:**
- Limited to Miami-Dade, does not extend to Broward or Palm Beach
- Marginal impact on healthcare access patterns

**SunRail (Orlando):**
- Commuter-focused, limited hours/coverage
- Does NOT meaningfully reduce healthcare friction

**All other metros:** Negligible transit for healthcare purposes

### Geographic Barriers

**Water Barriers:**
- **Tampa Bay:** Bridges create friction between Tampa and St. Petersburg despite proximity
- **Charleston Harbor:** Peninsula geography limits crossings
- **Intracoastal Waterway (Florida):** Creates east-west friction in coastal communities
- **Mississippi River:** Splits Memphis metro
- **Tennessee River:** Creates friction in Chattanooga, Knoxville areas

**Coastal Geography:**
- **Florida Keys:** Connected only by Overseas Highway (US-1), 3+ hour drive from Miami
- **Outer Banks (NC):** Limited bridge access, seasonal congestion
- **South Carolina Low Country:** Barrier islands create distinct markets
- **Florida Panhandle:** Linear coastal development, not integrated east-west

**Terrain:**
- **Appalachian Mountains (TN/NC/GA):** Create hard barriers for east-west travel
- **Great Smoky Mountains:** Separate east Tennessee from western North Carolina
- **Blue Ridge Mountains (NC/SC/GA):** Isolate mountain communities

### Weather and Seasonal Factors
- **Hurricane season:** June-November creates evacuation route congestion (I-75, I-95, I-10)
- **Summer tourist traffic:** Florida coast, North Carolina mountains, Tennessee
- **Snowbirds:** Seasonal population spikes in South Florida, Southwest Florida

**Atlanta:**

**Miami-Fort Lauderdale-West Palm Beach:**

**Tampa-St. Petersburg:**

**Charlotte:**

## Critical Notes for This Region

### Car Dependency Reality
Unlike the Northeast and Mid-Atlantic, transit infrastructure does NOT reduce friction in the Southeast. Do not assume:
- MARTA collapses Atlanta suburbs
- Tri-Rail collapses South Florida
- Any commuter rail reduces healthcare friction

The ONLY friction reducer is highway access, and sprawl + congestion create massive barriers within large metros.

### Sprawl Is the Dominant Factor
Atlanta, Miami, Orlando, Tampa, Charlotte all have extreme sprawl. Key principle:
- **Population size does NOT determine market count**
- **Geographic size + congestion determines market count**
- Atlanta needs MORE markets than Boston despite similar population

### HCA Dominance
HCA Healthcare (headquartered in Nashville) operates 50+ hospitals across the Southeast. However:
- HCA presence does NOT automatically integrate markets
- Residents still follow 45-minute rule for routine care
- HCA facilities often compete with each other across market boundaries

### Florida Is Unique
- Enormous population (22M+) spread along linear coasts
- Expect 15-25 markets in Florida alone
- Do NOT consolidate coastal segments just because they're in same state
- I-95 and I-75 are not "integration corridors" — they're just roads with congestion

### Military Installations
Large military bases exist throughout region, but:
- Do NOT count military hospitals as anchors for civilian markets
- Military bases may create separate sub-markets (e.g., Fort Campbell near Clarksville TN)

Apply the 45-minute rule rigorously. The Southeast has extreme sprawl and car-dependency creating friction WITHIN metros, not just between them. Err on the side of splitting markets, especially in Atlanta, South Florida, and other sprawling metros.
